Your first small exploration

One useful observation is enough.

You are trying out a way of looking at prose. You do not have to improve anything today.

1 · Open the kit for your AI

Unzip the kit. Put the stylo-starter folder inside a folder called Writing Practice. Open Writing Practice in the AI tool you already use. Keep the kit name for this first try so the prompt can find it.

The AI can keep its practice-session folder beside the kit. The setup page covers tool access and what happens if Python is missing.

2 · Copy this request

Copy this into your AI
Read stylo-starter/machine/START.md in this workspace and follow its first-look workflow.

Help me try the included demonstration before using my own writing. Check whether you can run the measurement tool. If you can, create a separate practice-session folder beside the kit and run the demo into a new report folder there. Don't download books or build an author profile.

Give me the HTML reading page, explain only a few useful observations, and ask one small question at a time. Keep the AI records in Markdown. If you cannot run the tool, show me the precomputed example and say clearly that it is the included result.

What you should get: an HTML page comparing the two included passages, a few observations, and one gentle question. The AI should tell you whether it ran a new comparison or opened the included example.

3 · Tell it what you notice

Copy this into your AI
I like [a detail or feeling you noticed], but I'm unsure about [anything that feels off]. Help me connect that reaction to the passage. Don't turn the numbers into rules or rewrite anything yet.

Replace the bracketed parts with whatever you can say. “A feels easier to read, but B feels closer to her thoughts” is useful. So is “I don't know why I like it.”

That counts as a win.

You looked at writing, noticed a pattern, and made room for your own reaction. There is no score to pass.

4 · Try a sample of your own, if you want

Choose a copy of an older or disposable passage. A short sample can introduce the tools; it does not establish the limits of your whole voice.
