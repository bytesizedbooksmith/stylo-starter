# At the gate — sample B

The spare key was still in the blue cup beside the gate, where their mother had insisted it was safe because nobody looking for a key would think to look in something so obvious. Mara closed her hand around it and stood listening to the faint clatter of dishes from inside, relieved that her brother had arrived first and irritated with him for making her feel relieved.

When he opened the door, he had the cherry-patterned dish towel over his shoulder. She almost reached for it. For years he had been the one who needed feeding, fetching, reminding, and now he was standing in their mother's doorway as though there were practical things left to do and he could do them.

He said there was tea. Mara said she did not want any, then noticed the bare strip beneath the radiator where their mother's shoes had always waited, toes turned toward the door.

“Half a cup, perhaps.”

He stepped aside without asking her to explain. She kept the key in her pocket as she passed him; returning it to the cup would have meant deciding something about whether she belonged here, and for the moment she wanted only to sit down.
