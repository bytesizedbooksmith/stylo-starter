# At the gate — sample A

Mara found the spare key in the blue cup. She picked it up. Put it down. Picked it up again.

“You're early,” her brother said.

“The bus was early.”

“It never is.”

“Well. Today.”

He stood in the doorway with a dish towel over his shoulder. Their mother's dish towel. The one with the cherries. Mara wanted to take it from him. She wanted him to keep drying the dishes so she would not have to.

“I made tea,” he said.

“I don't want tea.”

“All right.”

He moved aside. She stepped in. Nothing had changed, except the shoes were gone from under the radiator. That was enough. She stared at the clean strip of floor.

“I might have half a cup.”

He nodded once, as if half a cup were a thing he knew how to manage.

Mara left the key in her coat pocket. She was not ready to put it back.
